Battery life—the invisible tether that governs every laptop owner’s daily routine—just got stronger in **Windows 11**. Microsoft’s **January 2026 Patch Tuesday** update, **KB5074109**, finally addresses the notorious **modern standby** **battery drain** that has plagued NPU-equipped devices since version 24H2 rolled out. The fix? Ensuring **Neural Processing Units** actually **power down** when **idle**, rather than secretly sipping battery like an unwelcome guest who never leaves.

> Microsoft’s KB5074109 update finally stops NPU-equipped laptops from draining batteries overnight by ensuring Neural Processing Units actually power down when idle.

For anyone running Windows 11 on hardware with an NPU—increasingly common as manufacturers bet big on AI capabilities—this update marks the end of **overnight battery depletion**. The issue affected both 24H2 and 25H2 systems, where the NPU stubbornly remained powered on during idle states, turning what should have been efficient standby into a slow-motion battery drain. KB5074109 corrects this behaviour, ensuring the NPU shuts down properly when you’re not actively using AI-accelerated features.

But Microsoft didn’t stop at fixing broken components. The company revamped **default power settings** for **S3-capable devices**, those machines still supporting traditional sleep instead of modern standby’s always-on connectivity.

Screen timeout now defaults to three minutes on battery and five when plugged in. Sleep kicks in after ten minutes unplugged, fifteen otherwise. These tighter defaults mean less idle power waste, though users can adjust them in Settings > System > Power & battery.

Energy Saver mode, often overlooked in the quest for performance, automatically activates at **low battery levels**—customisable to whatever percentage makes you anxious. It dims the screen, throttles background processes, and typically prioritises survival over speed. **Quick access** via the battery icon in the notification area means toggling it doesn’t require navigating through menus as your battery gauge approaches zero.

Beyond these headline changes, Microsoft introduced granular optimisations that stack into meaningful savings. **Dynamic Refresh Rate** drops to 60Hz when your laptop detects you’re not gaming or scrolling rapidly. **Dark Mode** reduces power draw on OLED panels.

The **powercfg /sleepstudy** command—a diagnostic tool that sounds more intimidating than it is—reveals which apps drain battery during standby, exposing the culprits keeping your machine awake when it should be resting.

Background app management matters more than realise. Checking **power usage metrics** and revoking background permissions for apps that don’t need them creates noticeable improvements. **PC Manager**, Microsoft’s optimisation utility, automates some of these tweaks while suggesting charge limits between 80-85% to extend **lithium-ion battery longevity**.

These changes arrive as users increasingly expect laptop **battery life** to match their phones—not in raw hours, necessarily, but in predictability. Modern standby promised smartphone-like instant wake without the **battery anxiety**. Turning off [Wi-Fi and Bluetooth](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/power-settings-in-windows-11-0d6a2b6b-2e87-4611-9980-ac9ea2175734) when not in use provides additional battery savings that complement these system-level improvements.

KB5074109 and accompanying optimisations suggest Microsoft finally understands that this promise requires more than marketing jargon. It requires components that actually sleep when instructed. The update focuses on [improving overall system performance](https://www.windowscentral.com/microsoft/windows-11/windows-11-kb5074109-january-2026-security-update-available-now-npu-battery-life-fix) alongside enhanced security measures for Windows 11 devices. These improvements are essential, especially given the ongoing [Windows 11 USB typec support issues](/windows-11-pressure-usb-type-c-support-oems/) that have frustrated many users. As devices increasingly rely on USB Type-C for connectivity and power delivery, resolving these issues will be crucial for maintaining user satisfaction and productivity. With the latest update, Microsoft aims to address these challenges and ensure a smoother experience for all Windows 11 users.
