Is your WiFi slow? If so, chances are that your Netgear WiFi router might not be placed in the right location. WiFi doesn’t travel far, especially through concrete and metal. Therefore, it is crucial to find out the best place for your Netgear router.

**Yes, Your Router Placement Matters a Lot!**

It’s alluring to stick your WiFi router, without thinking much, wherever you find space in your home, but that’s a mistake. A WiFi signal travels so far, and the more walls, furniture, or other materials it comes across, the shorter that distance becomes. In the event that the internet appears slow or spotty on your smartphone, tablet, or PC, check your WiFi signal strength and channel interference. Slow WiFi will also prevent you from configuring your Netgear router via **routerlogin net** web page. [Expanding wifi coverage in small homes](/how-to-expand-the-internet-coverage-of-your-wifi-connection/) can often be achieved by strategically placing your router in a central location or using extenders to boost the signal where it’s weakest. Additionally, minimizing interference from other electronic devices can significantly enhance your internet experience. By ensuring that your WiFi is optimized, you can enjoy uninterrupted streaming, gaming, and browsing.

On the off chance if you find you don’t have a stable WiFi signal in particular areas of your house, even on clear channels, it’s time to move your WiFi router. It might not be the best place to reach every corner of your house.

**Keep the Router in a Central Location**

You might have known the fact that the central area of your home is the best place for your Netgear WiFi router. However, it is not always the truth. For example, if a kitchen, garage, or living room includes most of your first floor, then putting the router in the center area of that floor might deliver more coverage in your garage instead of the areas you use most. In addition to optimizing your router’s location, understanding the [top countries with highest internet usage](/which-countries-use-the-most-internet/) can provide valuable insights into connectivity trends. Countries like China and India lead the way, showcasing how varying habits can influence the demand for better home network solutions. This information can help you anticipate potential issues and take proactive measures to ensure your WiFi remains strong and reliable.

On the other hand, if the closest area to the center of your home is the kitchen, following the ‘center of home’ advice could also be harmful because some appliances such as microwaves can interfere with WiFi signals incoming from your router.

Ultimately, your WiFi router needs to be near the center of the most important area of your house. Aim for the center of a location where you want the fastest WiFi speeds. No matter whether you have employed the **Netgear AC750 setup** or any other model setup, the central location is the best place to keep your router.

**In a Multilevel Home, Consider the Ceiling and Floor**

If your home has multiple floors, consider which floors you use the most to enjoy internet surfing on your devices. In the event that you use two floors the most, the best placement to cover both stories is either the ground of the higher floor or the ceiling of the lower floor.

Do not hide your Netgear wireless router in a drawer or behind books. The WiFi signal from the router has to penetrate the floors and walls of your house without adding more barriers. So, place your WiFi router accordingly.

**Keep the Router Away from Interference-Creating Devices**

There are several devices in the home which can create interference in the path of WiFi signals coming from your router. Such devices include electrical gadgets, reflexive surfaces, metal objects, water resources, etc.

Therefore, make sure to keep your Netgear router far away from microwaves, refrigerators, Bluetooth speakers, cordless phones, baby monitors, fish tanks, mirrors, glasses, metal doors, aluminum studs, treadmills, electrical garage openers, and then some. Otherwise, you may come across **router login** problems.
