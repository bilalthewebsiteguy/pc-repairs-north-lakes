Windows 11 has achieved a major milestone, surpassing **50% market share** across global Windows PCs by mid-2025, with approximately 53% of users now running Microsoft’s latest operating system. This change comes in spite of initial resistance and compatibility challenges, particularly in enterprise environments. As Windows 10 support ends in October 2025, the **migration pressure** intensifies for remaining holdouts, suggesting **Windows 11**‘s dominance will only strengthen as organizations navigate the inevitable shift.

![windows 11 market dominance achieved](/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/windows_11_market_dominance_achieved_afzj8.jpg)

After years of gradual adoption, **Windows 11** has finally crossed a major milestone, surpassing **50**% **market share** across **Windows PCs** globally by mid-2025. The operating system’s rise to dominance marks a significant change in the Windows ecosystem, reaching approximately 53% of Windows PCs by **August 2025**, while **Windows 10**‘s share dropped to 43%.

> Windows 11 finally claims majority share of global PC market, marking a turning point in Microsoft’s latest operating system journey.

This milestone didn’t come easily for **Microsoft**. The journey to majority status was marked by initial **user resistance**, **compatibility hurdles**, and **enterprise hesitation**. **Early adoption rates** were sluggish, with many users content to stick with Windows 10, viewing its successor as more of a cosmetic upgrade than a necessary evolution. Microsoft’s aggressive push through pop-up notifications and upgrade prompts often backfired, creating user pushback rather than enthusiasm. Recent data shows a concerning trend as [Windows 11 adoption stalls](https://www.theregister.com/2025/06/03/windows_11_market_share/), with market share actually dropping from 43.72% to 43.22% in June 2025.

The **corporate world** has been particularly cautious in embracing Windows 11. Large enterprises, traditionally conservative in their IT decisions, have largely maintained Windows 10 as their standard operating system. This stance is driven by concerns over **application compatibility** and the desire to minimise disruption to business operations. The requirement for [TPM version 2.0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windows_11) has been a significant barrier for many organizations with older hardware infrastructure.

Nevertheless, with Windows 10’s **end-of-life** approaching in October 2025, many organisations are now accelerating their **migration plans**.

Regional adoption patterns tell an interesting story, with some markets leading the charge as others lag behind. The **United States** has emerged as a front-runner, with Windows 11 claiming the majority share of Windows desktops. Surprisingly, Guyana stands out as an early adopter, becoming one of the first countries where Windows 11 dominated the market. These variations reflect differences in **hardware refresh cycles**, corporate policies, and local tech ecosystems.

The statistics paint a clear picture of Windows 11’s ascendancy. From holding less than 40% market share in late 2024, the operating system has steadily climbed to its current position. This growth has come primarily at the expense of Windows 10, though small but persistent pockets of older versions like Windows 7 remain, particularly in industrial and government sectors.

Looking ahead, Windows 11’s dominance seems set to strengthen further. The impending end of Windows 10 support will force many holdouts to upgrade, as improving hardware compatibility and enterprise-focused features are making the change more appealing.

Nonetheless, the path forward isn’t without challenges. Corporate IT departments continue to weigh the benefits of upgrading against the risks to **mission-critical applications**, and some users still find Windows 10 perfectly adequate for their needs.

The milestone represents more than just numbers – it signals the beginning of a **new era** in the Windows ecosystem, one where Windows 11 will increasingly define the standard for **desktop computing**. As the operating system matures and its feature set expands, its position as the dominant Windows version appears increasingly secure.

## Final Thoughts

With **Windows 11** now running on over **50% of Windows PCs** worldwide, this shift marks a significant change in the landscape of PC computing. As user acceptance grows and Windows 11 establishes itself as the new standard, developers will prioritize optimizing their applications for its features. This transition signals the beginning of the gradual phase-out of Windows 10, which will reach its **end-of-support date** in 2025.
