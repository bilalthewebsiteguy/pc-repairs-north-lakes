**Windows 11’s** Default Services**: The** Silent Speed Drainers\*\*\*\*

Windows 11 runs a staggering 100+ background services, quietly siphoning your PC’s **performance**. Each service can rob gamers of 2% to 5% FPS, turning a smooth experience into a stuttering affair. Imagine playing your favorite game only to have your fun blunted by unnecessary processes. Disabling non-essential services has proven to free up precious RAM and boost performance. Curious about which services to axe for peak speed? The answer may surprise you. [Windows 11 user growth statistics](/windows-11-surges-past-one-billion-users/) indicate that more gamers are experiencing these performance hitches. As the user base expands, the demands on system resources increase, making it crucial for players to optimize their setups. With so many people jumping on board, understanding how to streamline Windows 11 can mean the difference between victory and defeat in competitive gaming.

![optimize windows 11 performance](/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/optimize_windows_11_performance_ojp7y.jpg)

While **Windows 11** may appear to be **optimised** for top-notch performance, several **default services** can quietly **drain** your PC’s speed. Take Virtualisation-Based Security (VBS), for example. With its default activation, VBS aims to protect the kernel from **malware** by employing virtualisation techniques. However, independent testing indicates that it can reduce **gaming performance** by anywhere from 5% to 25% in CPU-intensive situations. Imagine launching your favourite game only to discover that your frames per second (FPS) have plummeted; disabling VBS might be the solution for reclaiming those lost frames. Of course, turning it off could compromise system security, but for the casual gamer, is it a price worth paying for a **smoother experience**?

Next, let’s discuss **GPU Scheduling**, also known as Hardware-Accelerated GPU Scheduling (HAGS). Oddly enough, it remains disabled by default in Windows 11. Why? Enabling HAGS allows the GPU to take charge of scheduling tasks, potentially resulting in smoother performance. [Enabling HAGS can provide minor performance gains, especially for systems with 10GB+ VRAM.](https://hone.gg/blog/optimize-windows-11-for-gaming/) However, this may increase **VRAM usage** by about 1GB—an insignificant amount for many but a deal-breaker for others. The caveat? Not all games or hardware configurations may experience benefits from it. So, is this enhancement similar to that one friend who brings snacks to game night but eats half of them themselves?

Then there are **power plan settings**. The default **Balanced plan** may save energy, but it can throttle CPU clock speeds, ultimately hindering performance. For those wanting to unleash the full power of their CPU, switching to a **High Performance** or even **Ultimate Performance** plan could be transformative. These plans can significantly enhance performance, especially in CPU-intensive tasks, making a noticeable difference when it counts most. Notably, [Windows 11 requires specific hardware](https://www.go-euc.com/windows-10-to-windows-11-what-is-the-impact-nowadays/) to optimize these performance configurations effectively.

Moreover, Windows 11 runs a multitude of **background services** and **telemetry**—over 100 by default. Each of these can chip away at your **FPS**, reducing it by 2% to 5% during gaming. As Windows 11 transmits approximately 12% more network data than its predecessor, those who prioritise performance must weigh the potential gains against the increased IOPS and network activity. Essentially, disabling non-essential services can free up RAM and reduce **CPU spikes**, much like decluttering your digital workspace. In addition to these considerations, users should also be aware of the [windows 11 activation features explained](/windows-11-game-changing-activation/), as these can affect system performance and user experience. Utilizing effective activation methods can streamline your setup process, ensuring you have access to all the latest features. By leveraging these activation options, you can enhance productivity while mitigating unnecessary resource drain on your computer.

Additionally, services like **Windows Search** indexing can place considerable demands on CPU and RAM, especially during updates. While its instant search capabilities are convenient, users who seldom utilise it might find disabling the service liberating.

Lastly, we should not overlook **startup programs** and **bloatware**. Default installations often launch numerous apps upon booting, leading to sluggish startups and higher RAM usage. Regularly auditing to disable unnecessary startup applications could be the proverbial ‘easy win’ for **performance enthusiasts**.
