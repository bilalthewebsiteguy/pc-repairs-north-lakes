Microsoft is quietly sunsetting V3 and V4 printer drivers on **Windows 11**, halting new publishing approvals on **Windows Update** after January 15, 2026. Your current printer won’t suddenly stop working—existing drivers stay functional—but installing that same model on a fresh machine could become problematic. The shift, triggered by **PrintNightmare security nightmares**, pushes manufacturers toward **Windows Protected Print Mode** and universal class drivers. Legacy hardware faces an uncertain runway, with feature improvements ending July 2027, so IT departments might want to audit their fleet now before upgrade headaches multiply. As users transition to the latest operating system, they may encounter issues such as unexpected crashes, particularly related to graphics processing units. To address these concerns, exploring various [windows 11 gpu crash solutions](/windows-11-update-gpu-crashes-wpa3-failures-fixes/) is essential for maintaining optimal performance. It’s advisable for IT departments to stay informed about potential fixes and updates to ensure their systems run smoothly in this evolving landscape.

Microsoft is **discontinuing support** for **legacy printer drivers**, and your office workhorse from 2015 might not make the cut. The company announced in September 2023 that **V3 and V4 printer drivers**—the backbone of thousands of models currently operating in offices worldwide—will stop receiving new publishing approvals on **Windows Update** starting **January 15, 2026**. This isn’t an overnight disaster, but rather a **gradual transition** aimed at **modernising Windows printing** without causing widespread panic.

The changes will affect Windows 11 versions 24H2 and 25H2, along with **Windows Server 2025** and beyond. When the **non-security update** KB5074105 is released in January 2026, followed by February’s security patch, the rules will change quietly. New V3/V4 drivers won’t be available on Windows Update unless vendors go through a **manual review process** on a case-by-case basis. **Existing drivers** already installed will continue to function normally. However, if you try to set up the same printer on a new machine, you may run into issues. As users navigate these updates, they may encounter various [windows 11 update issues for users](/windows-11-kb5063878-update-ssd-failures/), particularly when attempting to install new drivers or troubleshoot existing devices. These complications could lead to frustration, especially for those who rely heavily on their printers and peripherals in their daily tasks. It’s important to stay informed about potential fixes and workarounds to minimize disruptions during this transition period.

Interestingly, most users won’t notice a thing. Modern printers already utilise newer frameworks and IPP inbox class drivers that easily meet **Microsoft**‘s updated requirements. The company’s phased rollout over several years aims to minimise disruption. However, **multi-function printers**—those all-in-one devices that handle faxing, scanning, and copying—reportedly face more challenges during upgrades. Legacy models that rely solely on V3/V4 architecture have uncertain futures unless manufacturers provide updated packages.

> Modern printers will transition smoothly, but multi-function devices and legacy models relying on outdated architecture face an uncertain future.

Why the change? **PrintNightmare vulnerabilities** exposed serious **security flaws** in the Windows print spooler, prompting Microsoft to take action. Managing thousands of vendor-specific drivers had become an unsustainable burden. The solution was to shift responsibility back to manufacturers while encouraging a move towards **Windows Protected Print Mode** and **universal class drivers**. This is modernisation dressed up as security improvement, but the rationale is sound: fewer attack vectors, improved performance, and a cleaner architecture. The transition aligns with Microsoft’s [broader strategy for](https://www.windowscentral.com/microsoft/windows-11/windows-11-finally-pulls-the-plug-on-legacy-printer-drivers-starting-january-2026) Windows 11 modernization, enhancing overall system security and stability. As users transition to this updated system, it’s crucial to address potential issues such as [windows 11 explorer crash solutions](/windows-11-explorer-crashes-taskbar-fix/). Many users may experience instability during this adjustment period, leading to frequent crashes that can hinder productivity. To mitigate these problems, Microsoft is expected to release patches and updates to optimize performance and enhance user experience.

What should you do? Start by contacting your printer manufacturer. Most have already transitioned to supported driver formats or provide downloadable **OEM installer packages**. Navigate to Settings > Devices > Printers & scanners, download the latest version, and restart the Print Connector service after installation. Print a **test page**. Done.

If your vendor has gone silent and the printer predates the Obama administration, it may be time for a replacement. Not ideal, but **legacy hardware** eventually reaches its end of life.

The broader takeaway is that Microsoft isn’t destroying printers—it’s setting clear boundaries. Windows 10 21H2 began this journey when manufacturer installers started disappearing. Now the company is finishing what it started, betting that most of the market has already moved on. Those still holding onto decade-old hardware will receive a **grace period** measured in years, not months. By July 1, 2027, third-party driver updates delivered through Windows Update will be [limited to security fixes](https://www.inkl.com/news/microsoft-purges-windows-11-printer-drivers-putting-millions-of-devices-on-borrowed-time-legacy-printers-face-extinction-as-microsoft-stops-distributing-v3-and-v4-drivers), effectively ending feature enhancements for legacy models. Adjust accordingly, or prepare for difficult conversations with IT about why accounting can’t print invoices anymore.
